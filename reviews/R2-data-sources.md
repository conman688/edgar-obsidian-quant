# Extend the screener with filings already free

The most valuable additions to edgar-obsidian-quant are not exotic alternative data. They are more SEC data handled with the point-in-time (PIT) discipline the repo already has, plus a small macro and factor layer. Ranked by value against effort, the order is: (1) insider selling and option-exercise signals from Form 4 rows the database already stores, (2) 8-K event flags such as auditor changes, non-reliance and officer departures, (3) FINRA short interest and days-to-cover, (4) a macro-regime overlay from FRED/ALFRED and Cboe VIX history, and (5) Fama-French/AQR/q-factor returns, so that backtests report alpha after factor exposure rather than raw returns. Next come 13F institutional holdings, Schedule 13D/13G stakes, Form 144 sale notices, late-filing (NT 10-K) flags and the Financial Statement and Notes text tables, which are all free, bulk and dated by filing. Sentiment and alternative data (GDELT news tone, Wikipedia pageviews, USAspending awards) belong at the bottom. They are cheap to try but weak for a stock screener, and some popular sources (Reddit, StockTwits, Google Trends via pytrends) are unreliable or barred by their terms. Before any new signal, the most important fix is the repo's own weakness: by its own count, 57% of filers have no price history, so every backtest overstates results. A survivorship-free price source, which is likely paid, would improve every signal below. Several facts in this report were seen only in search summaries or come from background knowledge, and they are flagged where they appear.

## The repo covers XBRL fundamentals and insider buying, not much else

The repo is an XBRL-fundamentals and Form 4 insider-buying screener backed by DuckDB, with PIT backtests and an Obsidian vault sync. It pulls four SEC sources: **`companyfacts.zip`, `submissions.zip`, the ticker/exchange file, and Form 4 data** (the quarterly Insider Transactions Data Sets plus `form.idx` and per-filing XML for recent quarters) ([config](https://github.com/conman688/edgar-obsidian-quant/blob/main/src/config.py); [form4_sources.py](https://github.com/conman688/edgar-obsidian-quant/blob/main/src/ingestion/form4_sources.py)). XBRL facts are limited to periodic forms (10-K, 10-Q, 20-F, 40-F and their amendments), so **8-K data is never loaded** ([companyfacts.py](https://github.com/conman688/edgar-obsidian-quant/blob/main/src/ingestion/companyfacts.py)). The insider signal uses only **code `P` open-market purchases made outside 10b5-1 plans** ([queries.py](https://github.com/conman688/edgar-obsidian-quant/blob/main/src/db/queries.py)). Text inputs are 10-K Item 1A risk factors scored with a Loughran-McDonald tone count ([filing_text.py](https://github.com/conman688/edgar-obsidian-quant/blob/main/src/ingestion/filing_text.py)) and the related-party footnote from the 10-K XBRL instance. That footnote feature (milestone M10) was merged on 2026-11-22, and its first live run was still pending ([ROADMAP](https://github.com/conman688/edgar-obsidian-quant/blob/main/ROADMAP.md)). Prices come from Yahoo Finance through yfinance, which the README labels unofficial ([README](https://github.com/conman688/edgar-obsidian-quant/blob/main/README.md)).

The PIT rules are the repo's real strength, and every addition should copy them. A screen sees only facts with **`filed <= as_of`**. A fact whose period ends more than 7 days after its filing date is ignored. A restatement becomes a new row under the new filing ([README](https://github.com/conman688/edgar-obsidian-quant/blob/main/README.md)). The SEC client stays at **8 requests per second against SEC's 10 per second cap** and refuses to run without a User-Agent containing a contact email ([sec.py](https://github.com/conman688/edgar-obsidian-quant/blob/main/src/ingestion/sec.py)). This matches SEC's stated policy: a 10 requests/second maximum, a declared company name and admin email, and a statement that SEC "reserves the right to limit request rates" ([SEC accessing EDGAR data](https://www.sec.gov/os/accessing-edgar-data)).

There are three known gaps. First, facts refresh only weekly (Saturdays), while SEC republishes the bulk zips nightly at about 3:00 a.m. ET ([SEC EDGAR APIs](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces)), so a weekday screen can run on facts up to a week old. Second, the ROADMAP itself flags survivorship bias, only about 180 picks over 15 years, and SIC codes and tickers taken from a current snapshot rather than history. Third, the repo does not read 13F, 13D/13G, 8-K items, Form 144, NT 10-K, S-3/424B dilution filings, DEF 14A pay data or Forms 3 and 5. The repo was read through the GitHub API, not cloned, and `screen.py`, `backtest.py`, `pit.py` and `forensics.py` were not read line by line. GitHub metadata gave a last update of 2026-11-09, which conflicts with the 2026-11-22 M10 merge in the ROADMAP. Check the live repo before writing integration code.

### Rank 1 costs nothing to ingest

Sell (`S`), option exercise (`M`), tax withholding (`F`) and gift (`G`) rows, plus the `is_10b5_1` flag, are **already stored but unused by any screen** ([schema.py](https://github.com/conman688/edgar-obsidian-quant/blob/main/src/db/schema.py)). Useful variables include cluster selling outside 10b5-1 plans, net insider dollar flow over 90 days, and a "buy while peers sell" contrast. The repo already notes the main trap: the 10b5-1 flag does not exist before April 2023, so older sells cannot be split into planned and discretionary. SEC added an AFF10B5ONE element to the 2023–2025 data sets in July 2025 ([SEC insider data sets](https://www.sec.gov/data-research/sec-markets-data/insider-transactions-data-sets)). Forms 3 and 5 sit in the same data sets and are unused. Form 5 catches late-reported and exempt transactions.

### 8-K events are the biggest gap in coverage

data.sec.gov serves submissions JSON with no API key, and SEC says XBRL processing typically takes "under a minute" ([SEC EDGAR APIs](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces)). Auditor changes (Item 4.01), non-reliance on prior financial statements (Item 4.02), impairments (2.06), delisting notices (3.01) and officer departures (5.02) are classic red flags. A value screen that hunts for cheap stocks needs them most, because cheap stocks include the value traps. There are two caveats, both unverified. The item numbers and the four-business-day filing deadline come from background knowledge, because the SEC Form 8-K PDF would not render, so check them against [the form](https://www.sec.gov/files/form8-k.pdf). It is also unconfirmed whether the submissions JSON carries item codes for each filing across full history. Pull a sample file before designing the table.

### Short interest is the cheapest positioning signal

FINRA posts short interest twice a month as CSVs covering exchange-listed and OTC stocks. It publishes **on the seventh business day after settlement**, so data is about two weeks old when it appears. The free archive before 2014 covers **OTC securities only**, and the files are for "non-commercial use of data" ([FINRA short interest files](https://www.finra.org/finra-data/browse-catalog/equity-short-interest/files)). Days-to-cover is short interest divided by average daily volume ([FINRA glossary](https://www.finra.org/finra-data/browse-catalog/equity-short-interest/data/glossary); search snippet only). In a backtest, key each value to the publication date, not the settlement date. A May 2026 filing, **SR-FINRA-2026-012, proposes weekly reporting** ([FINRA filing](https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/2026-05/SR-FINRA-2026-012.pdf)). Whether SEC approved it is unverified, so design the ingester so the reporting cadence is not hard-coded. Daily Reg SHO short-sale volume files go back to August 2018 and post by 6 p.m. ET on the trade date ([FINRA Reg SHO](https://www.finra.org/finra-data/browse-catalog/short-sale-volume-data/daily-short-sale-volume-files)). They measure short-sale volume, not open short positions, and include market-maker shorts closed the same day. Weight them well below short interest.

### Macro and factor data mostly make backtests more honest

FRED supplies regime inputs such as the **10-year minus 2-year Treasury spread (T10Y2Y)** ([FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/T10Y2Y)) and the **Chicago Fed NFCI financial-conditions index**, which is weekly ([FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/NFCI)). For revised series such as GDP and payrolls, ALFRED's `realtime_start`, `realtime_end` and `vintage_dates` parameters return values as they were known on a past date ([FRED API docs](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/docs/api/fred/series_observations.html)). The most attractive credit series is a trap. **The ICE BofA high-yield spread (BAMLH0A0HYM2) holds only 3 years of history on FRED since April 2026**, and ICE prohibits reproduction without written permission ([FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/BAMLH0A0HYM2)). Use it for live monitoring only, never as stored backtest history. Cboe publishes free daily VIX history from 1990, plus VVIX, VIX9D, OVX and GVZ, and states the data is "furnished without responsibility for accuracy" ([Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/tradable_products/vix/vix_historical_data/)). The VIX9D-to-VIX spread is a free proxy for the volatility term structure. VIX futures history and MOVE appear to be paid.

Factor files are where the repo gains the most for the least work. Kenneth French's library offers 3- and 5-factor, momentum, reversal and industry files as CSV zips. It also states that **full histories are recomputed each month and can change when CRSP revises its data** ([French library](https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/data_library.html)). AQR adds Quality Minus Junk, Betting Against Beta, and Value and Momentum Everywhere ([AQR datasets](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Datasets)), and global-q.org has q-factors from 1967 to 2025 ([global-q](https://global-q.org/factors.html)). A cheap, low-EV/EBIT screen with positive free cash flow will load heavily on value, quality and small size. Regressing its monthly returns on these factors shows how much of the result is alpha and how much is ordinary factor exposure. Save a dated snapshot of every download so the regressions can be reproduced.

### Ownership filings are worth a medium build

The SEC's 13F data sets run from July 2013 and are posted quarterly ([SEC 13F data sets](https://www.sec.gov/data-research/sec-markets-data/form-13f-data-sets)). Holdings are quarter-end positions filed up to 45 days later, so **the filing date is the known-at date**. Using the quarter-end date leaks up to six weeks of future information into a backtest. SEC also refreshed all 13F sets in March 2024 to fix missing records, so a download made today differs from one made earlier. The table layout is documented in a readme PDF that was not read. Under the 2023 amendments, Schedule 13D is due within five business days and amendments within two, with structured XML filing from December 18, 2024 ([SEC press release](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2023-219); search summary only). Mixing pre- and post-2024 filings therefore mixes two different reporting lags. Form 144 notices (affiliate sales above 5,000 shares or $50,000 in three months, per [Investor.gov](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/glossary/form-144); search summary) signal an intention to sell, not a completed sale. They pair naturally with the Form 4 sell signal from rank 1.

## Sentiment and alternative data rank last for this screener

GDELT is the only free, long-history news source found with a timeline API. Its DOC 2.0 window starts on 2017-01-01, and article lists cover only the last three months ([GDELT blog](https://blog.gdeltproject.org/doc-2-0-updates-1-5-year-searching-and-updated-mobile-interface/)). That source is a 2018 post, and the rate limit of about one request per five seconds comes from a third party. GDELT keeps adding late articles, so re-running a query can return different values, and the repo would need to save its own snapshots to keep backtests reproducible. Wikipedia pageviews are cleaner: daily, CC0 licensed and not revised, from mid-2015 ([pageviewapi](https://pypi.org/project/pageviewapi/)). Sources disagree on whether the start date is July or August 2015. USAspending is keyless, offers bulk CSV downloads and has an `awards/last_updated` endpoint ([USAspending API](https://api.usaspending.gov/docs/endpoints)), but it matters only for government contractors.

Several sources are not worth building on. Third-party summaries report that **Reddit's free tier is for non-commercial use, that new public API requests stop on 2026-10-31, and that public access ends in March 2027** ([data365](https://data365.co/blog/reddit-api-limits)). This is unverified because Reddit's own terms could not be fetched. StockTwits has no confirmed free official tier, and its terms bar scraping ([StockTwits terms](https://stocktwits.com/terms); not fetched). pytrends was reportedly archived in April 2025 and fails often with HTTP 429 errors ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/esteban_ortega/pytrends-is-dead-heres-how-to-get-google-trends-data-in-2026-1a18)). PatentsView appears to have moved to the USPTO Open Data Portal in March 2026 ([USPTO notice](https://www.uspto.gov/subscription-center/2026/patentsview-migrating-uspto-open-data-portal-march-20)), and the sources conflict on its current access rules. Earnings-call transcripts, job postings, app downloads, web traffic, ETF flows, securities-lending borrow fees and the OCC and FINRA OTC Transparency pages were either not researched or failed to load. **No free source was verified for any of them.**

## Tooling choices carry licensing and stability risk

**edgartools 6.4.0** (MIT, released November 24, 2026) is the most relevant library for 8-K, 13F and ownership parsing ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/edgartools/)). It moves fast across major versions, so pin it in `uv.lock`. **yfinance 1.8.0** says the Yahoo API "is intended for personal use only" and that the library is not affiliated with Yahoo ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/yfinance/)). The repo's price layer is therefore both a licensing risk and the main source of survivorship bias. Free survivorship-free price data is scarce: Quandl's free WIKI dataset reportedly stopped updating in 2018, and Stooq could not be checked. Paid options such as Sharadar are reported at about $9–39 a month for personal use ([QuantRocket](https://quantrocket.com/pricing/data/sharadar)), a vendor claim that is unverified. **fredapi was last released in May 2024** ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/fredapi/)), so calling the FRED REST endpoint directly is a safer choice. OpenBB 5.0.0 lists Apache-2.0 on PyPI ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/openbb/)), but GitHub mirrors still show AGPL ([OpenBB licensing](https://docs.openbb.co/platform/licensing)). Read the LICENSE file before adding it as a dependency. pandas-datareader 0.11.1 is maintained but narrower in scope ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/pandas-datareader/)).

For the vault, the inferred design (not sourced from Dataview documentation) is to add `Sources/` notes recording licence, rate limit, key requirement and PIT caveat, and `Variables/` notes recording unit, frequency and known-at rule. Every observation should keep **`as_of` (when the value became public) separate from `period` (what it describes)**. That split is the same rule as `filed <= as_of`, written down where a human reviewer can see it.

## Conclusion

The best next step is defensive. The rank-1 insider-sell query, 8-K red-flag events and factor attribution would make the existing screen more trustworthy before they make it broader. Its likely weak spots are value traps and returns that are really factor exposure, and these three additions target exactly those. Every source in the table has its own "known-at" date that differs from the date it describes: FINRA's publication date, the 13F filing date, ALFRED's vintage date and a GDELT snapshot date. The repo's `filed <= as_of` rule generalizes to all of them only if each ingester records its publication timestamp at ingest time.

The limit no free data can remove is the price layer. With most filers missing price history and only about 180 picks in the sample, adding twenty more signals mainly adds ways to overfit. A modest paid survivorship-free price source, plus the free SEC and FINRA files above, would do more for decision quality than any sentiment feed. Before building anything that depends on them, verify the open items: the 8-K item map and submissions `items` field, the 13F table layout, SEC's decision on weekly short-interest reporting, and the OpenBB licence.
